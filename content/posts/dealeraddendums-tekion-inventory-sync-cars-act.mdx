## Why Real-Time Inventory Sync Matters

A new car arrives on your lot. Someone has to print a window sticker, an addendum, and a Buyers Guide before it can go on the line. An **addendum** is a dealer-added sticker that lists extra costs, like paint protection or a market adjustment, next to the factory price.

If your inventory system and your sticker-printing software don't talk to each other, someone has to type in the VIN by hand. That takes time. It also creates room for mistakes, like a wrong price or a missing disclosure.

Real-time sync fixes this. It means your inventory management system, like Tekion, sends vehicle data straight to your sticker-printing software the moment a car is logged in. No retyping. No waiting.

## Understanding Tekion Integration and the New Inventory API

Tekion is a cloud-based dealer management system (DMS). A DMS is the main software a dealership uses to track inventory, deals, and customer records.

Tekion offers a **New Inventory API**. An API (application programming interface) is a set of rules that lets two computer systems share data automatically. When a dealer adds a new vehicle in Tekion, the New Inventory API can send that vehicle's VIN, trim, and price data out to connected partner software right away.

DealerAddendums connects to this API. That means when your sales team logs a new unit in Tekion, DealerAddendums can pull the vehicle details and prepare an addendum template without anyone re-entering data. ## California's CARS Act (SB 766): What Changed

In October 2025, California passed a new state law about vehicle pricing. It is called the CARS Act, short for Consumer Automotive Recovery, Safety, and Fairness. It is formally known as **Senate Bill 766, Chapter 354, Statutes of 2025**. Governor Newsom signed it on October 6, 2025. The law becomes operative on October 1, 2026.

The CARS Act applies to licensed California dealers selling light-duty vehicles under 10,000 pounds gross vehicle weight. That covers nearly every car, truck, and SUV sold at a typical dealership.

The core idea behind the law is upfront pricing. Dealers must show the full, true price of a vehicle, including add-ons, instead of advertising a low price and adding fees later at the desk.
